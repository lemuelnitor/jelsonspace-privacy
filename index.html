Jelson Space: Privacy Policy

Effective 19 November 2026

Jelson Space is a notes app for Android. This page explains, in plain language, what the app does with your information. The short version: everything you write stays on your phone, the app has no accounts, and the free version shows one small ad from Google that never sees what you write.

What stays on your phone

  • Your thoughts (their words, titles, formatting and dates) and your next steps (their days, times and what you told the app happened with them) are stored only in the app's private storage on your phone.
  • The categories you make, and which of your thoughts are in them, are stored the same way: only on your phone.
  • Pictures you add to a thought are stored on your phone only. They are never uploaded.
  • The app has no account or sign-in, so there is nothing to register and no profile about you.
  • Jelson Space has no analytics or crash reporting of its own, and it sends nothing to the developer. The only information that leaves your phone is the technical information described under Ads in the free version, below.
  • Text from pictures: when you choose Extract text on a picture in a thought, the printed words are read on your phone by Google's ML Kit text recognition, which is part of the app. The picture and the words are not sent anywhere by Jelson Space. The words are shown to you first, and only go into your thought if you choose Insert into the thought (or copy them).

Ads in the free version

Jelson Space is free. The free version shows one small ad at the bottom of the home screen, from Google AdMob. The first ad appears three days after you first open the app. Ads are never shown while you write or dictate a thought, in the editor, on the widget, or on any screen except Home.

What the ad software receives. When an ad is requested, Google's ad software inside the app, and Google, receive technical information from your phone:

  • your IP address (which can suggest your general location, such as your country or city);
  • your phone's advertising ID, a resettable number Android gives apps for advertising, and the app set ID (not sent if you have removed the advertising ID in Android's settings, or when only limited ads are shown);
  • which ads were shown and whether you touched them;
  • technical information about how the ad software performs, such as how quickly it starts.

Why. To choose and show an ad, to count that it was shown, to pay the developer for it, and to keep the advertising system safe from fraud.

Who receives it. Google, and the advertisers and ad partners Google works with when an ad is served. How Google uses this is described in Google's privacy policy and on its page How Google uses data when you use our partners' sites or apps.

What is never sent. Your thoughts, their titles, your categories, your next steps, your pictures, the words you dictate, the text read from pictures, and your backup files are never given to the ad software and never sent anywhere by Jelson Space. No ad is chosen from anything you write.

Your choice. Where the law asks for it (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), Google's consent screen asks you before the first ad. You can choose Consent, Do not consent, or Manage options. If you do not consent you still see an ad, but only a limited ad: Google describes limited ads as ads that do not use your advertising ID or personalise what you see. You can change your choice at any time in Settings, Ads, Privacy options (shown where the law requires it). Everywhere, you can delete your advertising ID or turn off ad personalisation in Android's own settings (Settings, Privacy, Ads), and manage Google's ad settings at myadcenter.google.com.

The internet. The app asks to use the internet only for ads and for the consent screen. Everything else, including writing, planning, dictation, reading text from pictures, and backup and restore, works without it.

Permissions, and why the app asks for them

  • Display over other apps, and a foreground service: for the optional floating button that lets you capture a thought from anywhere. It is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off in Settings.
  • Notifications: to remind you at a time you chose for a next step.
  • Internet, network state, advertising ID, basic phone state and keeping the phone awake for a moment: used by Google's ad software to show the ad and the consent screen, as described above. You can remove the advertising ID in Android's settings.
  • Start after restart: to put your reminders and the floating button back after your phone restarts.
  • Microphone: only when you tap the microphone, or press and hold the floating button, to dictate. Android's speech recognizer turns your speech into words and the app keeps only the words; the app does not record or store audio. Android's speech service is part of your phone, not of Jelson Space, and may use its own connection to recognise speech under the terms of that service.
  • Camera: only when you choose Take a photo in a thought. Android asks you the first time. The app then hands over to your phone's camera app, and the photo is stored on your phone only and never uploaded. If you say no, you can still add pictures from your gallery.

Backups and deleting your data

  • Android's automatic cloud backup is turned off for this app, so your thoughts are not copied to any account unless you choose to copy them yourself.
  • Back up my thoughts saves your thoughts, next steps, categories and pictures to one file, in a place you choose. The file is not locked with a password, so keep it private. Jelson Space never sends it anywhere; where it goes afterwards is up to you. Restore from a backup reads such a file and adds the thoughts that are not on the phone yet.
  • Deleting a thought removes it, its next-step history and any picture only it used. Removing the app from your phone removes all of its data.

Children

Jelson Space is for people aged 18 and over and is not directed to children. The app itself collects no personal information; the ad software is described above.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes how it handles information, this page will be updated first, and the effective date above will change.
